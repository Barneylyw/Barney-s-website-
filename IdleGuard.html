Introduction

The IdleGuard system is built with a team of 4 people during the E2C2 hackathon with the goal of implementing it onto a vehicle. The objective of this system is to detect when a vehicle has been idling for too long and sends a notification to the user’s phone via wifi. We used a IMU module MPU6050 to sense the motion, TMP36GT9Z for temperature sensing, and an ESP32 as the microcontroller and wifi connection.

Since this is a project involving multiple people, I will mostly cover what I have done in the project.

Overall Design

This section will give a rough outline of how we designed the system in (mostly) chronological order.

To design such a system, we first defined what sensors we need to sense the temperature, the motion, and sending notification to the phone, then we decided on where to put them within a vehicle. Our initial idea was to use a tilt sensor from the provided Arduino starter kit as the motion sensing mechanism by hanging the sensor on a pendulum, once it moves, that means the car have moved. But we realized an IMU would be more reliable and easier to implement in a real vehicle, so we pivoted to using my teammate’s IMU unit (MPU6050). I was in charge of this sub-project.

Then we discussed how we will detect if the engine is on, we thought of using a vibration sensor mounted on/near the engine block to detect its vibration and check if it’s running, this task was assigned to my teammate Yojith.

We unanimously agreed to mount the temperature sensor under the oil pan to detect if the car is warmed op enough to drive, my teammate Clark took up this part of the project.

Since Amitoj had previous experience with ESP32 and IoT systems, he will be working on the notification system.

At some point I started asking the question, ‘how will we get power for this system?’ (this turned out to be more important than I thought it would be) After a brief discussion with Clark and explanation to Amitoj and Yojith, we agreed to tap the power off of the alternator, and if we have time in the competition, we will build a buck converter and use AD2 to simulate the power from the alternator. This decision turned out to be more important as we decided to assume the user will not turn the auxiliary on without turning the engine on. Meaning we don’t need to detect if the engine is running, and Yojith is out of a job now (demoted to assisting Amitoj on coding the notification system).

Since Amitoj need to know when to have send the notifications, we then had a discussion about the possible states in the mechanism; to visualize and simplify the process, we used a truth table to determine what state we care about, I believe Yojith was programming all the states. IMU System Design

This section focuses on what I did for the IMU sub-system, including the wiring between MPU6050 and ESP32, and the test code.

To design the IMU system, I first determined what pins are available on the ESP32, and specific breakout board. On the MPU6050, I found I only need to use its I2C and power pins, I figured that I2C on the module would go to I2C on the ESP32, and the power pins would go to the VDD and GND of the system.

In order to confirm my wiring, I used an online guide and datasheet to verify my wiring. The guide used an Arduino Uno instead of an ESP32, but it showed the SDA and SCL pins on the module going to the SDA and SCL pins on the Arduino. I determined that my wiring is correct since both ESP32 and Arduino are microcontrollers and need data from I2C, and I wired the ESP32 the same way they wired the Arduino.
